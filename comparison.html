What the calculation includes

Annual total = 12 monthly premiums + modeled cost sharing. Cost sharing is the smaller of (a) the out-of-pocket maximum and (b) the remaining deductible paid from eligible charges plus coinsurance on charges above the deductible. All figures assume one individual, in-network covered allowed charges, and a fresh plan year with no prior spending.

Premiums do not count toward the out-of-pocket maximum. This simplified comparison does not model copays, prescription tiers, separate family deductibles, preventive care exceptions, out-of-network bills, employer contributions, subsidies or plan-specific benefit exclusions. Your actual plan's Summary of Benefits and Coverage controls.

For authoritative explanations, see HealthCare.gov on total plan costs and out-of-pocket maximums. The official Marketplace individual maximum for 2026 is $10,600 and for 2027 is $12,000; these are limits, not assumptions about your specific plan.

Worked example

For $12,000 in allowed covered charges, Plan A's $6,000 deductible plus 30% of the next $6,000 yields $7,800 cost sharing. Add $3,600 in annual premiums for a total of $11,400. Plan B's $1,500 deductible plus 20% of $10,500 yields $3,600 cost sharing. Add $6,240 in premiums for a total of $9,840. Plan B is $1,560 lower in this simplified example.

Why the least-expensive modeled plan may not be the right plan

The calculation assumes every dollar entered as an allowed charge is eligible, in-network care subject to the same deductible and coinsurance rate. Actual plans can use separate drug deductibles, office-visit copays, tiered networks, noncovered services, and individual versus family accumulators. A plan with the lowest total in the table may exclude a preferred provider or medication.

Before enrolling, compare the official Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC), provider directory, prescription formulary, referral rules, and out-of-pocket maximum definitions. Use the checklist to record the differences.
