Insurance policy comparison checklist

Use this worksheet before trusting a premium-only comparison. Collect both quotes or policy summaries and enter the same coverage period. No personal information is submitted to this website.

1. Verify you are comparing the same protection

For each policy, record the insurer, policy term, covered people or property, major coverage limits, exclusions, and optional endorsements. A $1,000 deductible with a $100,000 coverage limit is not equivalent to the same deductible with a $300,000 limit. If one quote omits a type of loss, a numerical premium comparison cannot capture that difference.

2. Calculate the full premium

Record monthly or annual premiums and any installment fees. A $150 monthly premium costs $1,800 over twelve equal payments before fees. A $1,720 annual-pay quote might be cheaper even when the displayed monthly installment looks attractive. Check whether prices are introductory, conditional on discounts, or subject to renewal changes.

3. Identify deductible triggers

Ask whether the deductible applies per claim, per event, per policy year, or per person. Check separate wind, hail, hurricane, prescription, or out-of-network deductibles. A 2% wind deductible on a $350,000 insured dwelling value could be $7,000—not $2,000. Percentage deductibles depend on the policy's stated base.

4. Estimate realistic costs at multiple usage levels

Compare no-claim, modest-claim, and major-claim years. A higher deductible saves premium when no claim occurs but can increase immediate cash requirements after a covered loss. For health plans, include premiums plus estimated covered in-network cost sharing, and separately consider noncovered or out-of-network care.

5. Check the coverage gap

Compare the insured amount against a credible replacement-cost estimate. Replacement cost is not the same as market value or outstanding mortgage balance. Check sublimits for jewelry, electronics, water damage, and other categories. Ask about actual cash value versus replacement cost settlement and whether depreciation is withheld initially.

6. Verify the insurer's actual documents

Use the declarations page, policy contract, exclusions, endorsements, and for health insurance the Summary of Benefits and Coverage. If the quote and policy disagree, ask the insurer or licensed agent for clarification in writing. Save a copy of the final documents.
