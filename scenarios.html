Insurance decision scenarios

These hypothetical U.S. examples show how a result changes with the assumptions. They are educational calculations, not quotes, claims predictions, or recommendations for a specific policy.

Scenario 1: A cheaper premium, but a larger deductible

Plan A costs $1,800 a year with a $1,000 deductible. Plan B costs $1,400 with a $2,500 deductible. With no covered claim, Plan B saves $400 in premiums. With one $4,000 fully eligible loss and no other cost-sharing rules, Plan A's modeled total is $2,800 and Plan B's is $3,900. In this particular loss scenario, A costs $1,100 less.

What changes the result? A $500 covered loss is below both deductibles, so modeled totals become $2,300 and $1,900; B is $400 cheaper. Real policies may have exclusions, coverage limits, or coinsurance that invalidate the simplified model.

Scenario 2: A deductible expressed as a percentage

Suppose a property policy applies a 2% named-storm deductible to a $350,000 insured dwelling value. That deductible would be $7,000, even if the policy uses a $1,000 deductible for other covered events. A $5,000 eligible storm loss could produce no payment under the simplified deductible-only model. The exact calculation depends on the wording and covered event.

Scenario 3: Health insurance after part of the deductible is met

Assume a $6,000 allowed in-network charge, a $2,000 annual deductible, $500 already satisfied, 20% patient coinsurance after the deductible, and $5,000 of remaining applicable out-of-pocket capacity. The remaining deductible is $1,500. Coinsurance on the remaining $4,500 is $900. The modeled patient cost is $2,400 and the modeled insurer share is $3,600.

Important: An office-visit copay, a separate prescription deductible, a service exclusion, or an out-of-network provider could change this result. The maximum applies only to qualifying spending under the plan's rules.

Scenario 4: A property limit below replacement cost

A home has an estimated $350,000 replacement cost, a $300,000 limit and a $2,500 applicable deductible. Under the site's simplified eligible full-loss model, the insurer pays $297,500 and the remaining $52,500 is uncovered. This excludes coinsurance penalties, additional coverages, depreciation, and settlement conditions.

Scenario 5: Life insurance is a planning starting point

A household targets $65,000 in annual replacement income for ten years, plus $125,000 in debt and final expenses and $40,000 in goals. It already has $200,000 in available assets and existing coverage. The undiscounted estimate is $615,000. A detailed plan should consider inflation, survivor income, investment returns, benefit eligibility, and future spending changes.

How to use these scenarios

Change one assumption at a time in the calculators. Write down which policy provisions would need to be true for the estimate to apply. Then check the actual contract, not just the illustrated savings.
