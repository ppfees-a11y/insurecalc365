Insurance cost and coverage calculators

Compare what you pay, what you may owe after a loss, and where coverage limits could leave a gap. Enter your own policy figures—these tools do not generate insurance quotes or predict claims.

How to use these tools

Start with numbers from an actual insurance quote, declarations page, or benefits summary. A deductible is not the same as a premium; limits, exclusions, copays, coinsurance, and maximums can change what you owe.

These seven calculators are independent planning scenarios. They do not replace policy documents, licensed advice, or an insurer’s claim determination.

Before comparing insurance prices

Two quotes are only comparable when their coverage is comparable. Write down each policy's coverage limit, covered events, deductible basis, exclusions, copays or coinsurance, and the total premium for the same policy period. A cheaper quote can shift more risk to you.

Reading your results

A lower premium is not always a lower total cost. A policy with a larger deductible may reduce routine payments but require more cash after a claim. A coverage limit is the maximum payable under a particular coverage—not a promise that every loss is covered.

For policy decisions, compare the same coverage types and limits, read exclusions, and confirm whether deductibles apply per event or per year. See the full guide, worked examples, and formulas and assumptions.
